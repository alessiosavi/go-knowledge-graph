## Parchment conservation

The manuscript conservator Sobali oversees Velnora's parchment vault. Every manuscript used in the lesson consists of ordinary imitation sheets with invented writing. Sobali introduces the vault through a set of shallow drawers lined with clean cloth. Learners lift a sheet by its supporting board rather than pinching the corners. The conservator explains how repeated bending would make the imitation material harder to examine. Each board carries a simple outline showing where the sheet rested before it was lifted. Returning the board to that outline keeps neighboring sheets from overlapping. Learners describe wrinkles with small sketches and leave the material as they found it. They apply no chemicals, moisture, or heat. Sobali places a loose cloth over the drawer while the group discusses a sketch, then removes it for the next examination. This alternation encourages careful observation without prolonged handling. When the lesson finishes, the support boards slide into their drawers and the cloth edges are tucked away from the runners. The fictional manuscript conservator's responsibility is to preserve the arrangement and make each learner's observations understandable to the next group.

## Folio collation

For Velnora's scrambled folios, copperleaf names the collation remedy. The remedy uses folded paper booklets whose corner symbols were deliberately shuffled before the lesson. Learners spread the booklets on a wide felt surface with every symbol visible. A separate collation diagram pairs matching corners without reproducing any manuscript text. Copperleaf calls for comparing those pairs before moving a booklet, then nesting the matching folds from the outside inward. A mismatch is recorded as an unresolved pair and left beside the diagram. Learners explain why they believe a pair belongs together, allowing the group to identify an unsupported assumption. Once the folios have been nested, the outer fold is opened gently to check that no loose booklet was trapped underneath. The diagram remains next to the assembled paper until discussion ends. Participants can reverse every movement to reconstruct the original scramble. This makes the collation remedy a transparent classroom puzzle rather than a claim about restoring historical material. Copperleaf is an invented label for that puzzle and has no significance for any collection outside this fictional archive.

## Outgoing volume binding

Velnora binds its loan ledger with an indigo ribbon to distinguish outgoing volumes. The ledger contains invented book titles and blank spaces for classroom annotations. Its ribbon passes through two wide holes punched in a cardboard cover, leaving enough slack for the pages to turn easily. Learners compare this bound ledger with loose sheets that describe volumes staying on the shelf. They read the word indigo stitched onto the ribbon instead of relying entirely on its appearance. When an outgoing volume is represented by a wooden book prop, a learner places the prop beside the open ledger and draws its cover shape. The exercise asks the next learner to match that shape before moving the prop. An uncertain match stays beside the ledger for discussion. Nobody erases an earlier drawing to make the count appear complete. At the end, the props return to their storage crate while the ribbon remains threaded through the cover. The fictional loan ledger therefore preserves a visible connection between its outgoing entries and the indigo binding, even after all classroom volumes have been put away.
